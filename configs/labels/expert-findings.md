## ACL
High-grade partial or full-thickness tear: complete discontinuity or more than
half of the fibres disrupted, with or without pivot-shift bone bruises. Mild
signal change, degeneration or thickening without discontinuity is negative.
- Look: central sagittal slices from the femoral footprint (lateral notch
  wall) to the tibial insertion; coronal and axial through the notch.
- Tear: fibres not seen or interrupted, wavy or horizontal residual fibres,
  empty lateral notch on coronal/axial, fluid-bright mass replacing fibres.
- Indirect signs (raise, do not prove): bruises on the lateral femoral
  condyle and posterior lateral tibial plateau, anterior tibial translation,
  buckled PCL, deep lateral femoral notch, Segond fragment.
- Pitfalls: mucoid degeneration (thick, bright, but continuous fibres) is
  negative; an intact reconstruction graft is negative, a torn graft counts.

## MCL
High-grade partial or complete acute tear, with disrupted fibres and edema in
and around the ligament. Low-grade sprains and chronic or remote changes are
negative.
- Look: coronal slices through the medial joint line, femoral origin to
  tibial insertion; axial at the joint line.
- Tear: fibres interrupted or not seen, wavy, detached from bone, with
  bright edema in and around them on FS.
- Pitfalls: edema around intact fibres = low-grade sprain (negative);
  thick, dark, calcified ligament = chronic (negative).

## Medial Meniscus
Abnormal signal that definitely contacts the meniscal surface on at least two
images, or a morphologic abnormality (truncated, diminutive, displaced
fragment). Intrasubstance degeneration not reaching the surface is negative.
- Look: every sagittal slice through the medial compartment (posterior horn,
  body, anterior horn) and coronal slices through the body and posterior root.
- Tear: line of signal reaching the upper or lower surface on two or more
  images; blunted or missing inner edge; fragment in the notch (double PCL)
  or flipped into the recess; root gap ("ghost" meniscus); marked extrusion
  with a root or radial tear.
- Pitfalls: globular or linear signal that stays inside the meniscus is
  degeneration (negative); after meniscectomy a small meniscus alone is not
  a new tear.

## Lateral Meniscus
Same criteria as the medial meniscus, applied to the lateral meniscus.
- Look: every sagittal slice through the lateral compartment and coronal
  slices through the body and both roots. The posterior horn and posterior
  root are the commonest sites, especially with an ACL tear.
- Pitfalls: the popliteus tendon sheath passes next to the posterior horn
  and can mimic a tear; the transverse and meniscofemoral ligaments can mimic
  a tear at the horns. Follow the line across slices.

## Medial OA
A moderate or large area (about 1 cm or more across) of high-grade cartilage loss
(more than half the thickness) in the medial tibiofemoral compartment, with or
without marrow changes under it.
- Look: medial femoral condyle and medial tibial plateau on coronal and
  sagittal; cartilage is the thin intermediate-signal layer on the bone.
- Supports: bone surface exposed or nearly so over about 1 cm or more,
  subchondral edema or cysts under the defect, osteophytes, meniscal
  extrusion. Osteophytes alone or thin fraying are negative.

## Lateral OA
Same criteria as Medial OA, applied to the lateral tibiofemoral compartment
(lateral femoral condyle and lateral tibial plateau).

## PF OA
Same criteria, applied to the patellofemoral compartment (all patellar facets
and the trochlea).
- Look: axial slices through the patella and trochlea, central sagittal.
- Patellar facets and the trochlea are always PF, never medial or lateral.

## Effusion
A moderate or large amount of fluid distending the joint.
- Look: suprapatellar recess on sagittal and axial FS, plus the medial and
  lateral recesses on axial.
- Moderate or large: the suprapatellar recess is distended (convex walls,
  fluid clearly separating quadriceps fat from femoral fat), fluid extends
  into the side recesses. A thin stripe of fluid is small (negative).

## Synovitis
Inflammation and thickening of the synovial lining.
- Without contrast, look on FS sagittal and axial for: (a) a distended
  capsule whose lining is thickened or irregular, with intermediate-signal
  debris, fronds or septa inside the bright fluid, most often in the
  suprapatellar recess and behind the cruciates; (b) bright, ill-defined
  signal replacing the fat of the Hoffa fat pad, especially near its back
  edge.
- A simple effusion with a thin smooth lining is NOT synovitis. Hoffa signal
  alone is weak evidence.

## Baker's
A moderate or large fluid collection in the characteristic location behind
the knee.
- Look: axial FS at and just above the joint line, between the medial head
  of gastrocnemius and the semimembranosus tendon; confirm the neck between
  them; measure on sagittal.
- Moderate or large: clearly more than a thin sliver, roughly 2-3 cm or
  more in its long axis; a ruptured or leaking cyst counts.

## Contusion
A bone contusion: bone marrow edema-like signal from impact, without a
discrete fracture line.
- Look: every FS sagittal and coronal slice through both condyles, both
  plateaus and the patella; confirm dark on T1 if available (on non-FS PD
  or T2 marrow edema is hard to see).
- Bruise: bright, ill-defined, reticular or geographic marrow signal,
  usually reaching toward the subchondral bone. Typical patterns: lateral
  femoral condyle near the sulcus plus posterior lateral tibial plateau
  (pivot shift); medial patella plus lateral femoral condyle (patellar
  dislocation); direct-blow patterns.
- Do NOT count: thin edema under worn cartilage with OA, subchondral cysts,
  well-defined degenerative lesions, edema at a fracture line (that is a
  fracture), normal bright red marrow in young patients' metaphyses.

## Fracture
An acute cortical break or fracture line.
- Look: T1 / nonFS for a dark line through the marrow, with edema around it
  on FS; cortex of the plateaus, condyles, patella, fibular head, tibial
  spines; small avulsions (Segond at the lateral tibial rim, fibular head,
  tibial spine).
- Do NOT count: old or healed fractures, hardware, osteochondritis
  dissecans, subchondral cysts, chondral-only injury.
